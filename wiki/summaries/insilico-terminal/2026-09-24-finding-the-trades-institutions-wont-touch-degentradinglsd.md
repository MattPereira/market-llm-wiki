# Finding the Trades Institutions Won't Touch | DegentradingLSD

**Insilico Terminal** · 2026-09-24 · 1:03:57 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8GqZgOK9e78)

Former options market maker DegentradingLSD looks for trades that large funds cannot size or do not want to touch. His examples span discounted crypto trusts, distressed miners, memory stocks, and Zcash. The recurring condition is a market price shaped by forced selling, institutional constraints, or a gap in understanding; he pairs that search with cash reserves so a bad outcome does not end his ability to trade.

## Find the neglected trade, then survive it

**Mispriced options and trusts.** He entered crypto in 2019 after noticing that Deribit's Bitcoin options carried nearly the same implied volatility across strikes. Far out-of-the-money calls looked cheap to him, and a subsequent Bitcoin rally made his position about 100 times its initial premium, by his account. He regards the trade as a mix of mispricing and luck. Later, he bought Grayscale's GBTC and Ethereum trust shares at roughly a 50% discount to the underlying assets, once a feared Genesis liquidation had happened. A smaller investor could bear the uncertainty and could either hedge the crypto price exposure to isolate the discount or retain both exposures.

GBTC had previously traded at a premium because some institutions could buy a security but not Bitcoin itself. The trust did not then offer redemption into Bitcoin. When distressed lenders held large blocks of GBTC as collateral and needed dollars, anticipated selling pushed the shares below the value of their Bitcoin. Without redemption, arbitrage could not quickly close the gap. DegentradingLSD says the trust's 2% management fee also cost a buyer at a 50% discount roughly 4% relative to the capital invested. The discount was therefore compensation for real risks, not free money. He recalls Bitcoin futures briefly trading below spot as another sign of heavy hedging.

**Cash as staying power.** His options background taught him to consider the rare loss that wipes out a trader, even when a bull market rewards larger bets. He suggests holding some cash rather than moving wholly in or out of risk assets. In his example, a portfolio 60% invested loses much less than one fully invested if an asset falls 90%; the fully invested portfolio needs a tenfold gain to recover. He favors smaller fractions of a Kelly-sized bet because drawdowns can impair judgment, and cash lets a trader deploy into bear-market opportunities. As a portfolio grows, however, the small, overlooked trades that helped build it cannot absorb the same capital.

## Dispersion replaces the broad crypto trade

He expects **more divergence among crypto assets** than in prior cycles. Broad liquidity is not lifting everything together; remaining capital follows particular stories such as privacy, infrastructure, and AI. Bitcoin can move little while ZEC rises sharply. Retail traders seeking very large gains still gamble in small tokens despite fees and slippage. He sees a second, more durable source of speculation in crypto's capacity to move value, support payments between AI agents, and enable private model inference. Those future uses could draw capital to larger projects even without a general altcoin rally.

He treats the failed progress of the Clarity Act as less consequential than regulatory guidance that may still arrive. A Fed hiking cycle would constrain liquidity, though he argues higher rates cannot directly fix oil-driven inflation. At the same time, wealth created by AI companies could support risk taking. Because these forces conflict, he sees little trading edge in a single macro forecast. He instead asks which businesses can earn enough to tolerate high rates: high-margin growers rather than leveraged firms whose returns fall below their cost of capital.

That leads him to semiconductors, especially memory. He believes investors scarred by earlier chip cycles underestimate how long AI demand could keep memory scarce; he predicts a shortage lasting another five years. He names SanDisk, Micron, and SK hynix as examples. He also bought distressed crypto miners for their access to power, expecting their sites to become infrastructure for AI cloud operators. These are his forecasts and trade accounts, rather than demonstrated future returns.

## The instrument matters as much as the idea

For equities, he generally prefers a conventional broker such as IBKR: he says it offers deeper liquidity and lower fees than crypto equity perpetuals, leaving his crypto venue capacity for crypto ideas. An equity perp is not interchangeable with a share, nor are perps on different venues interchangeable. He cites an SK hynix perp on Hyperliquid whose oracle briefly reflected an anomalous opening-auction quote, causing liquidations that did not occur on Binance. Traders need to understand a contract's reference price and failure cases before expressing a view through it.

He is bullish on Nvidia despite its long flat stretches, expecting renewed appreciation as capacity-limited growth becomes harder to ignore. He compares it to an established compounder rather than a spectacular small-cap bet. He also thinks Meta and Google redirected buyback cash into AI infrastructure because they see high returns; he predicts their spending could eventually earn 30–50% annual returns on capital. Those estimates are his, and he acknowledges that many investors still doubt whether the spending will pay off.

## Privacy, Zcash, and crypto venue economics

DegentradingLSD doubts AI labs can collectively slow frontier development when each has an incentive to continue. He expects AI to make surveillance easier and sees privacy-preserving crypto and private inference as responses. Organizations using hosted models may expose valuable internal data; he is about 80% confident that private inference will become important, though he offers no specific token recommendation for that trade.

He bought ZEC during what he calls the May Ironwood exploit, when many privacy believers were selling. He argues that buyers willing to absorb that supply now hold coins tightly, leaving little spot supply and allowing large price moves. His account is a supply-and-positioning thesis, not a claim that Zcash's fundamentals alone justify its price. The host contrasts ZEC's belief-driven upside with HYPE's more measurable revenue and cash flow; DegentradingLSD agrees that different assets can lead in different market phases. He expects some AI inference tokens to gain ten- or twentyfold, but declines to name them.

He expects several perp exchanges to coexist if each finds a trading niche. In his view, Hyperliquid showed that meaningful token-holder value can win trust; excessive venture-capital allocations instead leave future buyers facing predictable selling. He hopes more teams will build for longer-term returns as venture investors become selective and AI lowers project-building costs. On crypto options, he expects use to rise as Bitcoin volatility and option premiums fall. Perps still offer retail traders more obvious leverage, and options venues must handle nonlinear risk, so he is unsure when options will become a breakout product.

## Into the midterms

After unexpected moves in oil, semiconductors, and momentum, he considers precise year-end calls unreliable. He thinks investors across crypto and traditional markets may be more exposed to downside than prepared for upside, while the political and oil problems he cites could be resolved. His practical position is patience: keep spare cash, avoid becoming too bearish, plan an exit when very bullish, and wait for mispriced opportunities rather than chasing small local moves.
