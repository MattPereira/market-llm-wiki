# MARKET UPDATE: Live With GMoney, Inflation Is In, Is This The Crypto Top?

**1000x** · 2026-09-11 · 40:55 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_xGXYvFHwc)

## NFTs after the winter

GMoney sees physical-card enthusiasm as evidence that collecting psychology never
left; younger, digitally native holders should eventually prefer assets they can
display on chain. Crypto wealth also creates demand for on-chain status goods, just
as gains elsewhere flow into watches, cars, and houses. He still owns CryptoPunks,
Chromie Squiggles, and Art Blocks.

His current example of “NFT 2.0” is **Quotrons**: a liquid token can be burned
one-way into an NFT, after which its holder receives trading fees as tokenized-stock
dividends. He presents that yield-bearing structure—not a simple collectible—as a
reason to hold rather than sell.

## The best on-chain month since DeFi summer

GMoney says the prior month brought the most interesting on-chain experimentation
since DeFi summer. The difference is the yield asset: instead of rapidly dumping a
farm token before becoming exit liquidity, users can receive tokenized Nvidia or
another stock they may want to own. He expects issuers to race for dominance in
tokenized equities as Circle and Tether did in stablecoins.

Memefi stock pairs help create demand and onboard newcomers, but neither speaker
treats the meme leg as investable on fundamentals. Avi argues the promise of a 30x
is unusually effective at getting first-time investors to fund wallets; GMoney uses
only small size because a missed exit can make him someone else's liquidity.

## Memecoins are PvP

They reject the idea that a memecoin can be diligenced: price depends on attention,
cult behavior, cabals, and timing. GMoney compares the market to Fortnite—participants
take pride in selling onto one another—whereas his trading-desk culture shared ideas
so a group could win together. Avi contrasts that PvP game with longer-lived PvE
narratives: even his profitable LUNA trade rested on adoption data later revealed as
fake, but the apparent thesis enabled conviction and staying power.

For both, public market discussion still has value. Long-duration theses have room
for many winners, while publishing invites better opportunities and challenges to
one's framework. Changing a position when facts change is risk management, not a
failure; frameworks are more useful than copying a trade that may already have
changed.

## AI, inflation, and the hike

After an in-line CPI print raised expectations of a rate hike, GMoney argues any
tightening cycle will be short. His framework: the US treats AI leadership over
China as national defense and will support the capex cycle regardless of near-term
inflation. Intervention while the S&P sits only about 5% below its high—and the
resulting bids in gold and Bitcoin—suggests markets already expect that support.

Avi reaches the same bullish AI conclusion from compute demand: power users want
more capacity, agents multiply consumption, and a one-point rate increase should
not stop spending with high expected returns. GMoney thinks any AI-capex contagion
would be contained faster than prior crises. Avi dismisses widening big-tech CDS
spreads because cash-rich Google, Microsoft, and Meta lack a bank-like balance-sheet
mismatch; GMoney notes that a much more leveraged future could make intervention a
political question.

Both are bullish Nvidia. Avi argues its financing of neocloud capex may create a
capital-light residual revenue stream if demand persists. GMoney holds longer-dated
calls on Nvidia, Micron, and Nebius, while recently devoting more attention to
smaller on-chain projects where modest positions offer faster convexity.

## Fake World Assets: own the casino

**Fake World Assets (FWA)** is an Ethereum gacha protocol: users pay to open an
on-chain blind box, while NFT owners deposit inventory and earn yield until an asset
is drawn. GMoney believes the shared liquidity layer could become the backstop for
other pack-opening products and unlock otherwise idle CryptoPunks, Bored Apes, or
tokenized physical cards. The key execution risk is distribution: more spins are
needed to sustain revenue. He quotes an approximately $12–13 million market cap but
is unsure whether a recent daily revenue figure was $30,000 or $300,000.

The broader bet is that social leaderboards and visible P&Ls will “FOMOify” more
casinos and financial products. If AI-driven unemployment increases gambling, they
would rather own the venues than depend on winning every game.

## Investing as national security

Avi argues AI will create enormous corporate wealth while leaving non-owners behind,
making broad market participation a social-stability and national-security issue.
Speculative products can be gateways, but he urges users to keep core Bitcoin and
Nasdaq exposure and limit leveraged gambling to roughly 5–10% of a portfolio.
GMoney's simpler rule: inactive traders should avoid leverage; active leveraged
traders need stops to survive.

GMoney closes with his disclosed on-chain bags: FWA and, most strongly, Quotrons.
He says Quotrons' next phase will bridge to Kraken's Ink network and create xStocks
pools whose fees support the project. At an asserted roughly $10 million market cap,
he sees favorable convexity—but the case includes speculative signals such as Kraken
CEO Arjun Sethi interacting with the account, not demonstrated adoption.

## Caveats

- Both speakers talk their books; GMoney explicitly names his positions.
- Revenue, market-cap, government-support, and future AI-wealth claims are stated in
  conversation and not independently verified.
- Their advice separates core ownership from gambling, yet many favored protocols
  depend directly on speculation, social proof, and continued participant inflows.
