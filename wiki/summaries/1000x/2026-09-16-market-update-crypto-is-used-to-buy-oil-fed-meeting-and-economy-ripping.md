# MARKET UPDATE: Crypto Is Used To Buy Oil?! FED Meeting, And Economy Ripping

**1000x** · 2026-09-16 · 1:04:46 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9pZhmQSroD4)

## Poland's $230 million oil loss: failure of diligence, evidence of adoption

Avi recounts a Financial Times investigation into Polish government-sponsored
traders attempting to buy Venezuelan oil. In his account, they handed brokers USB
sticks carrying access to USDT, received promises of oil and tankers, and lost
approximately $230 million when the intermediaries disappeared. He rejects the
headline's implication that crypto caused the loss: the failure was trusting people
whose access to oil had not been established.

For Avi, the overlooked story is that sovereign-backed commodity purchases were
being attempted with stablecoins rather than banks or cash. He calls this one of
the most bullish crypto-adoption stories he has seen, infers that many similar deals
exist beyond the exposed example, and expects stablecoins and on-chain settlement
to spread over the next decade. His investment implication is to own the companies,
producers, and users benefiting from that infrastructure.

## How oil scams work

Jonah draws on seven years at Vitol, where he ran a derivatives pod handling
physical–financial hybrids. Even outside the primary physical desks, he received
regular introductions to purported supply or offtake opportunities; none of those
unsolicited deals materialized. Oil's minimum economic ticket is usually tens of
millions of dollars. At quoted current prices, a Suezmax cargo represents roughly
$100 million and an ultra-large crude carrier roughly $200 million. This mature,
globally traded market is largely handled by major institutions.

Independent brokers can matter around sanctions and conflict, including the
Kurdistan and Russian-oil examples Jonah recalls. Local relatives of officials or
warlords may also need paying to move product. But he distinguishes those fees from
the actual oil purchase: most proceeds should reach a legitimate sovereign or
quasi-sovereign counterparty with documents and a bank account. A documented
transaction paid in Tether to a vetted address is plausible; a street-corner thumb
drive exchange is not. In his description of Venezuela's present situation, major
international traders are already involved, reducing the rationale for obscure
intermediaries.

Poland should have hired an experienced international oil trader, Jonah argues.
Avi adds that conventional finance also gets scammed: he recalls a former boss's
account of Merrill Lynch paying over $100 million for three nonexistent Indonesian
coal mines. Connections, physical inspection, and diligence matter regardless of
payment rails. Avi wonders whether entrusted traders could skim USDT, but explicitly
says the article provided no indication that these named traders acted unethically.
Both ultimately regard sovereign authorization of Tether payments as significant.

## Venezuela's new oil barons: who can actually participate?

Avi sees opportunity in the fragmentation of Venezuela's economy. He says Trump
criticized major oil companies for insufficient investment and speculates that
those firms fear future sanctions or political retaliation if Democrats regain
power. Private wealthy investors, including tech and crypto fortunes, might accept
risks the majors avoid. He initially suggests a young Spanish-speaking entrepreneur
could investigate opportunities locally and connect them with capital.

Jonah pushes back: a billionaire such as Fred Ehrsam may have the resources for
legitimate oil investment, but a typical 24-year-old does not. Oil's scale and
maturity leave little room without substantial industry experience and connections.
He favors smaller frontier commodities—minor metals used in batteries and chips,
for example—where shipments need less capital. Accounting services, imports, or
locally researched Venezuelan debt could also provide an edge. Avi accepts the
qualification and broadens his opportunity thesis beyond oil.

Jonah nevertheless sees buying bull-market dips in the right assets, potentially
with leverage, as the easier opportunity than building a frontier commodities
business. He expects the market to remain strong for some time.

## Treasury carnage and Canada's surprise

The hosts describe a sharp Treasury selloff; Avi says 20-year yields are reaching
levels not seen since 2008 and pushing up mortgage rates, even as retail sales remain
strong. His explanation combines higher prospective equity returns from AI-driven
productivity, heavy corporate borrowing, and Treasury-market intervention by Scott
Bessent. Investors consequently demand more compensation to hold government bonds.

Avi also sees unexpected competitive changes outside the US. He describes Canada
cutting business taxes and aligning with Europe, while Mario Draghi advocates
European data-center investment. In Avi's account, Europe has only about 5% of
global data-center capacity and should not import US overbuilding arguments into
an economy barely building any capacity. He views these moves as departures from
preservation and regulation toward investment and innovation.

If that direction persists, he expects additional pressure on the dollar and better
prospects for European and Canadian currencies over the following year. He says he
will not take the currency trade because he is not a forex trader.

## Copper as the next memecoin

Jonah's preferred AI-adjacent asset is copper: data centers need wiring, and he
believes supply is insufficient for the buildout. He has some exposure but feels
underpositioned, expecting a potentially parabolic move over several years. The
metaphor is that chips make silicon “think,” and copper connects that intelligence.
He argues this demand offers a longer, more durable opportunity than chasing
individual memecoins.

Avi prefers copper producers to the underlying commodity, arguing miners can expand
production while added supply restrains commodity prices. He favors **COPX**, a
basket of miners, to reduce individual-mine risk; Freeport-McMoRan also comes up as
a producer example. Jonah thinks this may be a case where owning the commodity
itself works too. They also raise possible anti-AI sabotage as a reason to avoid
concentrating exposure in a single mine.

## Meta challenges “pacing the frontier”

Avi praises Meta's Muse assistant and interprets Zuckerberg's “The Future Is for
Everybody” post as a rejection of coordinated restraint by OpenAI and Anthropic.
He says recent warnings from Sam Altman and Dario Amodei about catastrophic AI risk
had hurt chip stocks by suggesting slower model development and lower future
compute demand. If Meta keeps advancing, he argues, its competitors must follow:
the commercial prize from achieving broadly capable AI is too large to surrender.
This strengthens his bullish Meta thesis.

Avi considers near-term extinction fears overstated. Even an immediate AGI release
would take time to penetrate the economy; access to models alone does not supply
the implementation skill needed for damaging attacks. He points to the lack of
large-scale attacks in his examples and argues that safeguards and shutting down
models remain available.

His more credible risk is dependency: AI may become embedded throughout commerce
and infrastructure as the internet has, making disruption damaging even without an
autonomous system deciding to destroy humanity. He remains very long Intel and
bullish chipmakers rather than positioning for a compute slowdown.

## AI doomerism as a sales pitch—and a possible future crisis

Jonah interprets catastrophic warnings as a familiar fear-based sales tactic:
providers claim that only they can protect customers from the danger they created.
His economic argument is that open-source tokens cost roughly 1–10% of frontier
tokens, and trailing models running locally on a Mac Studio may soon satisfy even
intensive coding and language needs. That would undermine valuations of roughly
$2 trillion for frontier-model companies, perhaps leaving businesses worth closer
to $100 billion. In his view, existential fear helps defend their pricing and
fundraising story. Avi agrees with the sales-tactic framing.

Jonah compares cheaper intelligence with shale oil: a powerful deflationary force
that supports growth and reduces dependence on scarce resources. He retains an
approximately two-year horizon before superintelligence could introduce harder-to-
predict problems. Until then, falling intelligence costs should create substantial
value, supporting broad investment and buying dips with leverage sized to survive
them.

He does not dismiss all danger. He believes money printing and government backstops
have largely removed the possibility of another conventional credit crisis. The
next crisis could instead come from entrusting data, commerce, and infrastructure
to probabilistic software that behaves unpredictably, producing disruption akin to
severing internet cables. Physical conflict also comes up, though Jonah considers
an AI-driven financial crisis more plausible than human extinction.

## The rate hike, GP, and live Bitcoin positioning

Before the FOMC release, Avi reverses his prior expectation of no hike: strong data
and market pricing of roughly a 90% probability now lead him to expect 25 basis
points. Counterintuitively, he thinks a hawkish Kevin Warsh favors Bitcoin and gold.
His thesis is that tightening signals opposition to irresponsible fiscal policy
and Treasury intervention, but Bessent and the Treasury have more political power
and will ultimately prevail. He cites Trump's proposed $5,000 payments and disputes
the claim that they could avoid increasing the deficit. A dovish capitulation would
weaken this conflict-based thesis.

Avi says he bought ZEC the previous day and already sold it for a quick trade;
ZEC is up roughly 14% during the recording. He also discloses a large holding in
**GP**, a RuneScape-themed memecoin paired with gold. He says a 3% buy/sell tax
distributes gold to holders and has earned him approximately $5,000. The game's
economy taught him trading as a child, and the community has rekindled his enjoyment
of crypto. He owns 4.5% of supply, might sell some if market capitalization reaches
$100 million, and promises to put realized profits into the 1000X social token.
He explicitly discourages treating his enthusiasm as a recommendation to invest.

The hosts then report the expected **25-basis-point hike** live, with little
immediate reaction in BTC or ZEC. Avi attributes that to the decision already being
priced in and says the press conference matters more. He initially urges waiting:
Bitcoin had broken former support near **$76,500**, making some risk reduction
reasonable, though he does not advocate shorting.

As the reaction holds up, Avi calls gold a strong buy and reiterates his bullish
monetary-asset thesis. Jonah challenges the claim that higher rates are bullish,
recalling his mistaken dismissal of tightening in 2021, but agrees this particular
decision is largely priced in. Holding above roughly **$76,000** would give Jonah
confidence in a move toward all-time highs. Avi proposes a quick long targeting the
highs, with a stop below roughly **$74,800**; a very dovish press conference would
be grounds to exit under his framework.
