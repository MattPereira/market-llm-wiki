# Fed day!

**Kyla Scanlon** · 2026-09-17 · 2:54 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rWyuVx-V2uk)

## The hike

The Fed unanimously hiked rates, its first hike since 2023, right before the
midterms—and Trump posted on Truth Social that rates should be near 1%.

Scanlon frames the dual mandate—price stability and maximum employment—and rates
as a fire hose: when the economy runs hot the Fed turns it up, borrowing and
spending slow, and prices follow. Last fall it was cutting, worried about the
labor market. That market is still mostly stable, if narrow, but pressures have
built: the Iran war pushed oil up, diesel is near record highs, and the AI
buildout has become a competition for capital, with hyperscalers raising huge
sums in the bond market and squeezing everyone else.

## What a hike cannot do

It does not reopen the Strait of Hormuz or make more oil. Asked directly, Fed
Chair Kevin Warsh said the Fed cannot affect any individual price; what it can
do is stop the oil shock spreading into everything else and get inflation back
to 2%. The Fed's own projections do not show 2% until 2029—eight years above
target.

## Credibility

Credibility is what is really at stake, Scanlon argues; a president dictating
monetary policy invites a mess in a few years. In July, Warsh held rates steady
and refused to explain how he would get inflation down—he dislikes forward
guidance—and long-term yields rose and have not come back down since, for
reasons beyond the Fed. Part of this hike, she suggests, was proving the Fed
will act.

## What it means for you

More pressure on short-term debt and an immediate hit to credit card borrowers,
though the move was largely anticipated and should mostly be absorbed.

Separately, the EU's head wants Canada as the bloc's first associate
member—Canada, Scanlon reads, building an exit ramp from the American economy.
Both stories underscore how much an economy runs on trust: that the Fed will
decide correctly, and that America is a steady partner.
