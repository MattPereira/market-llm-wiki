# Steady Lads Live From TOKEN2049 in Singapore (2026)

**Steady Lads Podcast** · 2026-10-09 · 39:03 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5lMnN6rK4QE)

## Zcash leads a selective recovery

Looking back to the period before the October 10 crash a year earlier, the panel sees a changed hierarchy: older large-cap coins remain depressed, while Zcash and Hyperliquid have entered the top ten. Ethereum's roughly $4,400 price a year earlier contrasts with around $2,600–$2,700 now; Solana has roughly halved and Dogecoin has fallen about two-thirds, in their comparison.

**Taiki calls Zcash the coin of the cycle** and continues adding to both ZEC and HYPE as prices rise. He sees Bitcoin's unresolved quantum-computing risk as an overhang and Zcash's planned response, alongside privacy, as a reason it could compete for Bitcoin flows. His analogy is successive cycles in which Ethereum challenged Bitcoin and Solana challenged Ethereum. He expects Zcash to become a competing monetary narrative over the next several years.

## Crowded holdings need an exit framework

Justin says he sold 5–10% of his ETH near $4,850 and the remainder after the crash near $3,850. What felt late proved useful: exiting let him reset rather than remain attached to the old cycle. He subsequently held Lighter, recently sold that position, and still holds Zcash.

Taiki reports meeting fund managers across Asia and the US who all own HYPE, ZEC, Lighter, and NEAR. The question is whether consensus identifies the emerging winners or signals a crowded trade approaching local highs. Jordy says herding is normal, but valuations can run ahead of economics: buybacks become a small support relative to token prices as traders capitalize far too much future revenue. He uses HYPE and Lighter as examples.

Zcash has a different vulnerability. With no revenue anchor, slowing momentum can cause holders to leave one by one as the excitement fades. Jordy worries Taiki could surrender his gains and asks for concrete downside and profit-taking levels.

**Taiki's mental stop is ZEC below $1,000; he has no stop order set.** He would interpret losing four-digit prices as bearish rather than a clean buying opportunity, and acknowledges he could wake up to $300 ZEC. He expects to start selling above $2,000, including around $3,000–$4,000, while retaining a longer-term $20,000–$30,000 scenario. The panel compares that optimism with earlier Fartcoin targets and suggests closing perpetuals while retaining spot. Taiki replies that perpetual exposure also earns Variational points.

## Token launches compete with equity incentives

Justin, Variational's head of product, says its token generation event is planned for **Q4, with a 32% airdrop allocation**, versus the 31% he cites for Hyperliquid. He reports more than $4 billion in daily trading volume and expects Variational to be the year's only substantial launch. The others see a recurring pattern of major decentralized exchange tokens debuting near year-end.

They contrast this with hesitation at Base, Kraken's Ink, and Polymarket. Dim argues a public company such as Coinbase may gain more by keeping the prospect of a token alive: it attracts farmers, while an actual distribution can send them elsewhere and potentially hurt the stock. Polymarket has more flexibility because it is private and can use a token to raise capital with something resembling an equity narrative.

Jordy identifies a deeper conflict: **tokenholders and shareholders compete for the same economic value**. Giving a token fee utility or directing cash toward it can reduce the revenue reaching equity. A large token position on a company's balance sheet might compensate, but that tradeoff needs to work. A disappointing launch also risks damaging an established brand.

Taiki has farmed Ink but expects weak trading after launch. Other panelists are more hopeful about a proposed platform connecting trading and asset management, with BNB-like utility across its ecosystem. They discuss that vision as a possibility rather than demonstrated token economics.

## Pearl: an AI monetary narrative with heavy issuance

Jordy remains interested in AI-linked coins, citing gains in Grass and NEAR and renewed movement in Worldcoin. He expects attention around a possible OpenAI IPO to help the category. The newer focus is Pearl, described on stage as “AI Bitcoin.”

Justin heard repeated Pearl recommendations in Korea and bought because access was difficult: limited exchange availability and small transfers create friction that a larger listing might remove. Dim says the team is strong but warns about roughly **$1 million of daily issuance and supply potentially doubling over a year**. A listing also makes it easier for miners to sell; demand must absorb that supply continuously for the bullish trade to work.

Jordy describes a market barbell: revenue-producing venues such as Hyperliquid at one end and monetary assets such as Zcash at the other, with generic layer-one tokens less attractive between them. Pearl belongs to the monetary side. Its proposed proof of useful work uses AI inference computation for mining, with the hope that broad participation eventually produces a secure network and monetary premium.

He contrasts this with miners rotating among profitable coins and selling rewards into buyers. Democratizing mining access for people in DeFi could improve that structure, and he hints at work to make participation easier. The proposed security, adoption, and distribution benefits remain prospective.

## Crypto products converge on speculation

The panel's crab analogy describes products converging toward quick, stimulating bets, much as media converges toward short clips. Complex hedging or DeFi products struggle while games offering immediate gains and losses attract attention. Taiki's investment shorthand is to own the casino, such as BNB or HYPE, or its most attractive chips.

Jordy sees distinct games with durable audiences: newly launched tokens, memecoins, and trades built around visible personalities. A new listing can attract bettors regardless of quality; celebrity traders offer human stories to follow through success and collapse. Justin doubts that traders have broadly become celebrities, observing that the strongest claims tend to come from sponsored promoters.

**Tom Lee's ETH treasury strategy is a possible next expression of this behavior.** Dim discusses rumors that, after approaching a target of 5% of ETH supply, Lee could use remaining cash and influence to support Ethereum ecosystem projects and increase ETH per share. Jordy compares this with Paradigm building infrastructure around its existing ETH holdings.

If the treasury vehicle redirects spending from ETH accumulation toward partnerships, the panel considers a possible long in its shares against an ETH short, or longs in selected ecosystem assets against ETH. These are exploratory trades dependent on rumored capital allocation, not announced plans.

## Perpetuals venues may move into options

Justin alleges inflated volume at a prediction-market competitor's perpetuals product and says it withdrew from a planned trading competition. The panel distinguishes success in prediction markets from product fit in perpetuals.

Jordy likewise sees Hyperliquid's core perpetuals product as its strength, with prediction-market interfaces and other extensions yet to match that traction. Justin expects options to become the next competitive market in Q1. He thinks recreating an order book on-chain will be difficult and says Variational is exploring its request-for-quote model to bring traditional-finance options liquidity on-chain.

## Constructive outlook, selective entries

Dim expects continued separation between strong assets and the rest. He thinks Bitcoin bottomed near $60,000, wants the gold narrative to strengthen for a sustained move, and is waiting for better entry prices.

Justin sees many assets as fairly priced or expensive. Bitcoin's roughly 50% decline, versus deeper previous-cycle drawdowns, makes him doubtful of an equally explosive rebound. He likes Zcash, considers Hyperliquid a good but expensive product, and is looking for a new AI crypto opportunity.

Taiki expects markets to reward conviction in the right assets more than reflexively selling every rally. Jordy also thinks the bottom is in and remains long, citing renewed corporate Bitcoin purchases and a fear-and-greed reading around 70 versus roughly 9–10 earlier. He argues that waiting for an exact four-year-cycle bottom can leave investors sidelined as the market changes.
