# Will rising bond yields crash the market? (Or do you just need to close Twitter?)

**Naly** · 2026-09-25 · [read](https://bloomresearch.substack.com/p/will-rising-bond-yields-crash-the)

Naly holds a large share of wealth in digital assets and considers NEAR and ZEC underpriced. Rising Treasury yields squeeze corporate refinancing and make bonds more competitive with expensive AI equities. Higher rates also increase US debt costs. But those costs could push policymakers toward low rates and money creation, eroding cash and bond purchasing power. Naly sees both a market crash and currency dilution as risks.

## What Marks, Gromen, and Alden change

**Howard Marks** questions whether Treasury buybacks of long-term bonds solve anything if funded by shorter-term borrowing: the government still owes the money and must refinance sooner. AI infrastructure financing competes with Treasuries for capital, potentially keeping yields high and pressuring AI valuations. Marks does not expect an abrupt funding crisis or advocate a wholesale stock exit; he considers diversification while recognizing the strength of US businesses.

**Luke Gromen** argues that refinancing roughly $40 trillion of federal debt at higher rates makes inflation control harder. He expects growing interest costs to push policymakers toward financing the debt over defending purchasing power. If investors shift even a fraction of their bond holdings toward the smaller gold market, he expects a large gold repricing.

**[Lyn Alden's June newsletter](https://www.lynalden.com/june-2026-newsletter/)** complicates that defense: scarce assets and profitable businesses can protect purchasing power, but gold, Bitcoin, and AI stocks can still be overpriced. She favors gradual rebalancing from crowded assets toward less popular ones at reasonable valuations. Naly applies this warning to both entry price and the size of positions that have grown with the portfolio.

## Building a floor without abandoning upside

Naly continues moving some NEAR and ZEC profits into cash and gold while seeking other high-upside investments in physical AI and robotics. Cash funds spending and preserves flexibility; gold hedges monetary risk, though its price can fall. The aggressive side remains larger because Naly expects more recovery ahead. New Bitcoin highs would prompt a further shift toward cash and gold, but Naly is building that reserve now so a crash cannot force sales of long-term bets.
