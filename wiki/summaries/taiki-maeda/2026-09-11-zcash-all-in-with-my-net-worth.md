# Zcash: All-in with my Net Worth.

**Taiki Maeda** · 2026-09-11 · 34:59 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VK6DKuuqPWw)

## Thesis

Maeda calls ZEC his highest-conviction position and argues its move above $1,000
marks the start—not the end—of a repricing as an alternative store of value to
Bitcoin. His case combines a decade-long price breakout, increased shielded-pool
use, lower perceived regulatory risk than Monero, institutional access, and a
reflexive loop in which price appreciation attracts capital and deepens the
privacy set.

## Broader market view

- He rejects a guaranteed Q4 decline based on crypto's four-year cycle, though he
  treats widespread belief in it as a tradable, potentially self-fulfilling
  force. Having sold into the expected Q4 2025 rally, he bought during Q2–Q3 2026
  while others waited for a Q4 low.
- He believes crypto is early in a bull market but undergoing a K-shaped recovery:
  BTC, ZEC, and fee-generating buyback tokens such as HYPE and LIT can appreciate,
  while expensive infrastructure tokens without revenue continue to decay.
- His winner-selection heuristic works backward from the expected outcome. If an
  asset will rise 10–30x in a new bull market, he expects strength, improving
  fundamentals, and a persuasive narrative to be visible already—not an asset
  still trading 80–90% below its highs.
- Investors waiting in cash become future demand: they will buy a Q4 dip or chase
  once BTC clears their bullish threshold. Maeda expects that capital to favor
  assets already demonstrating strength.

## The ZEC flywheel

Maeda describes Zcash as a privacy-preserving store-of-value candidate whose
utility improves with adoption. More ZEC in the shielded pool enlarges the
anonymity set; a rising token price increases the value it can conceal. He argues
that this makes price, shielded value, perceived privacy, and new demand mutually
reinforcing.

He thinks the move above $1,000 matters because it normalizes four-digit prices,
clears a decade-long range, and takes ZEC above 1% of BTC's market capitalization.
From his stated ratio of roughly 1.6%, a narrative target such as 10% of BTC makes
several further multiples feel plausible to him. He compares this with silver at
roughly 13% of gold's value, while acknowledging that ZEC is not yet an established
store of value.

Potential marginal buyers include:

- Bitcoin holders diversifying a small allocation into ZEC for privacy or quantum
  risk;
- institutions seeking optional, purportedly compliant privacy rather than
  Monero's privacy-by-default model;
- traditional investors gaining access through a listed Zcash product; and
- crypto investors rotating from weak tokens into current market leaders.

## His answers to the bear cases

### Monero has more real privacy use

Maeda says current criminal use is the wrong investment lens; the bet is which
network a much larger future market will select. He favors Zcash's shielded pool
and optional transparency, arguing that funds left at rest become harder to link
as the pool grows. He characterizes Monero's ring-signature approach as more
exposed to future AI-enhanced chain surveillance and believes its regulatory
position limits institutional adoption.

### The rally is a coordinated narrative pump

He considers a supply corner implausible after ten years of proof-of-work issuance
and contrasts the breakout with DOGE and LTC failing to reach new highs despite
greater attention. He interprets ZEC strength during US market hours, growth in a
listed product he says exceeded $500M in assets, and rising shielded supply as
possible evidence of genuine demand. He cannot identify the buyers and explicitly
speculates that institutional filings may reveal them later.

### Bitcoin cannot have a store-of-value competitor

Maeda argues that Ethereum's L1 dominance gave way to multiple competitors, so
Bitcoin's historical lack of a rival does not prove one cannot emerge. In his
framing, Zcash may be crossing the adoption “chasm” after surviving multiple
cycles, reducing issuance, and differentiating itself through privacy and a
quantum-resistance roadmap.

## Positioning and process

- Maeda says he is “max long,” has bought ZEC both down and up, and now treats
  market confirmation as a reason to add rather than take profit. He remains long
  HYPE and LIT, trades perpetuals, and farms Variational.
- His portfolio test: imagine liquidating everything, then ask whether you would
  reconstruct the same allocation at today's prices. If not, cost basis or loss
  aversion is keeping you misallocated.
- “Locked in” means holding researched winners through volatility, not constantly
  buying new tokens from social-media promotion.
- His intended bull-market path is maximum risk early, followed by gradual
  de-risking as optimism and perceived safety rise.

## Caveats and tensions

- This is concentrated book talk. Price strength is repeatedly used as evidence
  that the asset is good and then the asset's quality is used to explain the price
  strength, making the argument substantially circular and momentum-dependent.
- Privacy quality, shielded-pool trends, listed-product assets, regulatory status,
  institutional buying, market-cap ratios, and Monero surveillance risk are
  asserted rather than independently demonstrated in the video.
- A larger shielded pool can improve anonymity, but price alone does not guarantee
  better privacy; transaction patterns, pool selection, wallet behavior, and
  protocol properties also matter.
- He admits he cannot explain the rally's buyers, forecast its horizon or top, and
  expects an eventual brutal correction. His 3–20% BTC-relative scenarios are
  narrative anchors rather than adoption-based valuations.
- In July, after an emotionally driven ZEC sale and repurchase, he emphasized cash
  as a psychological buffer and said his position was already large enough. He now
  describes himself as max long and still adding, a material increase in
  conviction and concentration as price rose.
- Maeda notes that his bullish targets historically tend not to be reached.
