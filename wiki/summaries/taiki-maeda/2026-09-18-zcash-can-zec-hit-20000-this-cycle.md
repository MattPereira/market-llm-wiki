# Zcash: Can ZEC hit $20,000 This Cycle?

**Taiki Maeda** · 2026-09-18 · 32:26 · [watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rF3wA0keKGw)

## Bull market pep talk

Maeda opens by rejecting persistent bear-market talk. He points to HYPE near $90,
LIT up roughly 3x from his entry a few months ago, and ZEC moving from ~$800 two
weeks ago to nearly $1,500. He frames this as a K-shaped market: most alts go to
zero, but a few assets run in their own secular bull markets without needing
Bitcoin to rise. Perp DEXes are his example—he believes perps keep taking share
from options, and HYPE and LIT plow most revenue back into their tokens.

He argues the doubters are funding because they hold cash and will have to buy
back higher, and when they do they will buy the winners, not Cardano. Winners
average winners; losers average losers.

## Zcash bull case

The pitch: ZEC as an alternative store of value to Bitcoin. Bitcoin's main
criticisms were programmability, scalability, and privacy. Ethereum and Solana
addressed the first two and delivered enormous returns; Zcash is the main asset
addressing privacy. He adds quantum risk—fears of a quantum computer reaching
Satoshi's coins—as a genuine bear case for Bitcoin, and notes Zcash is further
along its quantum roadmap. Bitcoin is ossified, which cuts both ways: predictable,
but unwilling to change, while Zcash has a developer community willing to ship
upgrades. He also finds Bitcoin culturally harder to root for than six years ago,
with Michael Saylor posting AI memes replacing the cypherpunk movement.

ZEC sits at roughly 1.8–1.9% of Bitcoin's market cap; he thinks that can reprice
to 10–30%.

## Price targets

Maeda grounds the targets in precedent from the 2017–18 block size wars, when the
market valued Bitcoin alternatives with far thinner differentiation:

- Litecoin—faster block times—peaked at 8% of Bitcoin's market cap and ranged 3–6%
  for about a year.
- Bitcoin Cash wicked to ~40% and ranged roughly 10–20% for nearly a year. People
  sold Bitcoin to buy it believing it was the better Bitcoin.
- Silver sits at ~13% of gold's market cap.

He considers Zcash far more differentiated than either fork was, so he brackets a
bear case at 5% of Bitcoin's market cap and a bull case at 20%, calling both
conservative and saying 50% wouldn't shock him. With Bitcoin at $100K—which he
treats as reasonable—5% implies ~$5,000 ZEC and 20% implies $20,000 ZEC. He notes
the framing that Zcash is two cycles behind Bitcoin, and Bitcoin hit $20K two
cycles ago. Most bear cases circulating online, he says, are based on what Zcash
was two to four years ago.

## Fundamentals

Zcash can be held in a transparent address (visible like Bitcoin) or shielded in
the pool, after which activity is undetectable. Maeda defines the fundamentals as
a function of price times shielded supply. For nine years the shielded pool did
nothing and so did the price; over the past year the pool has gone up only and so
has the price.

The mechanism is reflexive. If the shielded pool holds ~$100M, a billionaire
shielding $10M becomes most of the anonymity set and gets little privacy. As price
and shielded supply rise, the pool can support larger holders, so the product
literally improves as price rises—price is the fundamental, as with Bitcoin. He
argues this makes the move rational rather than a narrative pump: people are
buying on exchanges, withdrawing, and shielding.

Crossing $1,000 and 1% of Bitcoin's market cap normalized ZEC as a legitimate
emerging store of value. He sees reasonable Bitcoin maxis converting 1–10% of
their stack, and debasement-narrative buyers who once went 100% Bitcoin now
splitting perhaps 80/20. Those marginal flows strengthen both narrative and
fundamentals. He expects that at some point ZEC pumps hard and then drops 80–90%,
as Bitcoin did from $20K to $3K in 2018—hence the need for a profit plan.

## Understanding and monetizing risk

Maeda's core lesson: bet big early in a bull market, then take less risk over
time. The idea that you can wait for six-figure Bitcoin and then buy a 100x altcoin
is, he says, much harder than it sounds in hindsight. In 2022 he drew down ~50%,
held Bitcoin, ETH, and stables at the bottom, but lacked the nerve to add on the
way up while others called it an echo bubble.

He argues perceived risk and actual risk are inverse: when everyone fears a Q4 dump
and sits in cash, risk is lowest. Take the most risk in the first 10–30% of a bull
market and reduce through the last stretch. He was uncomfortable levering into
Zcash a month ago but stuck to the thesis.

## Profit-taking plan

For a narrative-driven asset with no cash flows, he uses a "next 2x" test against
the ZEC/BTC market cap ratio. 1%→2% took two weeks; 2%→4% is still not hard;
4%→8% is where it gets real, and Litecoin's 8% peak makes that a natural first
resistance.

His first take-profit target is 3–4% of Bitcoin's market cap, plausibly by year
end—with Bitcoin around $80K, that's roughly $2,400 to $3,200 ZEC. There he plans
to remove leverage and raise cash while holding spot bags untouched for long-term
capital gains; the leverage existed only because he thought the market was
offsides. Beyond that he rides toward 10–20% without assuming it must happen—
people who believed a Q4 pump *had* to come got destroyed last year. He notes
selling the dead-cat bounce rarely works either; last cycle's plan to sell Bitcoin
at $125K on the bounce never got the chance.

Upside catalysts he watches: a recent Zcash NFT mint that sold out, and Project
Tachyon shipping perhaps next year to improve scalability—Bitcoin ordinals failed
partly because maxis rejected them, but Zcash's community accepts change. He also
flags Bitcoin breaking $83–84K as the level where four-year-cycle bears capitulate
and buy whatever is hot.

## Trading style and position construction

Maeda prefers slugging percentage over batting average: being wrong often but
small, and right big. That means strong conviction, concentration, large sizing,
and strict risk controls—"it takes courage to be a pig." His Zcash trade came from
a six-month thesis; he sold the bottom in June, bought back sub-$500, then kept
adding at $600, $700, and $800 as Ironwood and the portal upgrade validated the
thesis. If your thesis is $10K and price goes $1K to $1.5K, you keep betting, not
sell. He admits he's uncomfortable buying more at current levels despite being
bullish.

The way people get wrecked is averaging down into things that are falling—usually
falling because they're bad tokens—and selling winners to fund losers, "pulling
out your flowers to water the weeds."

## Closing

Bears have been wrong for one to two months and are still offsides; when they
admit it they buy your bags higher. Bears sound smart, bulls make money. Size up
the top few ideas rather than the third-best one; the common error is undersizing
high-conviction ideas and oversizing marginal ones. He's ignoring the trenches and
holding large caps—HYPE, LIT, Zcash, some Bitcoin—plus farming Variational.
