# Big Tech data center bond financing

Researched 2026-09-29. Primary sources; completed borrowing distinguished from announced plans.

## How the financing works

Corporate bonds supply upfront cash; the issuer pays interest and repays principal at maturity. Senior unsecured bonds are obligations of the company, without specific property pledged as collateral. Investors therefore fund the company's credit rather than owning a particular data center. [SEC bond guide](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/what-are)

For Amazon and Alphabet, the disclosed purpose is general corporate funding. Bond proceeds expand the pool of cash available for construction, servers, acquisitions, and other spending; those filings do not establish that every borrowed dollar pays for data center construction. Meta describes infrastructure funding as coming from available funds, operating cash flow, and financing together. [Amazon Q2 10-Q, Note 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm), [Alphabet Q1 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000048/goog-20260331.htm), [Meta Q2 10-Q, liquidity](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm)

## Debt supplements operating cash

Amazon's first-half 2026 operating cash flow was **$71.4bn**, versus **$96.3bn cash capex**; capex primarily covered technology infrastructure, mostly AWS, plus fulfillment capacity. Meta generated **$64.09bn** operating cash flow versus **$49.11bn** cash purchases of property and equipment and **$50.92bn** capex including finance-lease principal. Alphabet's Q1 operating cash flow was **$45.8bn**, versus **$35.7bn** capex. These different periods and definitions should not be treated as comparable annual construction budgets. [Amazon Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm), [Meta Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm), [Alphabet Q1 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000048/goog-20260331.htm)

## Project financing: Meta Hyperion

Meta's October 2025 Hyperion venture is a different structure: Blue Owl-managed funds own **80%**, Meta **20%**, with approximately **$27bn estimated development costs** for buildings and long-lived power, cooling, and connectivity infrastructure. Some Blue Owl capital is financed through a private debt offering to PIMCO and other bond investors. Meta becomes the tenant: initial four-year operating leases, extension options, and a residual-value guarantee covering the first 16 operating years. The announcement does **not** disclose the private bonds' principal amount or coupon; $27bn here is the development budget. [Meta transaction announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/meta-blue-owl-capital-develop-hyperion-data-center/)

Meta's Q2 filing reports **$12.31bn** initial lease commitments beginning in 2029 and an approximately **$28bn** residual-guarantee threshold that declines over time. Conditional payments cover a shortfall between property value and the applicable threshold. Meta does not consolidate the venture. **Interpretation:** moving financing into a separate vehicle reduces direct ownership funding but retains economic exposure through rent and guarantees. [Meta Q2 10-Q, Note 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm)

## Oracle: bonds, equity, leases, customers

On February 1, Oracle **planned $45–50bn gross financing during calendar 2026**: approximately half equity/equity-linked financing, including an up-to-$20bn stock program, and half a single senior unsecured bond transaction. That announcement is a plan, not proof of completed proceeds. [Oracle financing announcement](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/oracle-announces-equity-and-debt-financing-plan-2026-02-01/)

By May 31, Oracle reported **$43bn debt + $5bn equity raised in fiscal 2026**, a different reporting window from calendar 2026. Operating cash flow was **$32bn**, free cash flow **negative $23.7bn**. Its June release projected another approximately **$40bn debt/equity financing in fiscal 2027**, including the $20bn stock program, while expecting no additional debt in calendar 2026. Prepaid and customer-supplied hardware portions of AI contracts totaled **$75bn**; this is not a $75bn cash receipt. [Oracle FY2026 results](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/q4fy26-earnings-release-2026-06-10/)

By August 31, the stock program was fully used: **$19.9bn net proceeds**, 141m shares. Oracle also disclosed **$288bn future lease commitments**, substantially all for data centers, generally starting fiscal Q2 2027–2029 with 15–19-year terms. These are future lease payments, not additional completed bond issuance or present-year capex. [Oracle Q1 FY2027 10-Q, Notes 6–7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389274/orcl-20260831.htm)

## What could go wrong

Existing fixed-rate coupons **do not reset when market interest rates rise**. Bond prices can fall and market yields rise while the issuer's scheduled coupon stays unchanged. Floating debt resets with its benchmark; new borrowing and refinancing face then-current financing conditions. [SEC corporate bonds](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/bonds-or-fixed-income-products), [SEC interest-rate bulletin](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-86)

**Inference from these structures:** the core risk is that AI capacity earns less or arrives later than expected while debt interest, principal, rent, and conditional guarantees remain. Long bond maturities postpone repayment; they do not ensure that today's chips remain useful for that long. For evaluating exposure, count corporate borrowing, project guarantees, leases, and committed equity separately; avoid adding development budgets to debt proceeds as though they were distinct borrowing. Meta's guarantee and Oracle's future leases show why corporate bond totals alone understate contractual exposure. [Meta Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm), [Oracle Q1 FY2027 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389274/orcl-20260831.htm)
